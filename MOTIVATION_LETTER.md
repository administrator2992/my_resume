## 3. How do you propose to address a development challenge in Indonesia?
*Character Count: 1373 (with spaces) | Limit: 2000*

Indonesia faces a significant digital divide. While major cities enjoy robust digital services, rural and remote (3T) regions suffer from unstable internet connectivity and limited power grid infrastructure. This digital isolation hinders growth in vital sectors like agriculture and disaster mitigation. Centralized cloud computing is often impractical due to high bandwidth costs and connection dropouts. 

My research addresses this challenge by optimizing Edge/Fog AI. Instead of relying on expensive, distant cloud servers, my proposal enables local, commodity devices (like single-board computers or old laptops) to run heavy AI models (e.g., YOLO for agricultural crop monitoring or PaddleOCR for digital services) efficiently. By managing hardware-level parameters (DVFS clock scaling and Core Affinity pinning), my framework maintains 100% model accuracy while reducing energy use and preventing device crashes.

Applying this in Indonesia will allow rural communities to run smart systems—such as real-time crop disease detection or early landslide warnings—locally and reliably, even with unstable power and networks. This reduces the cost of digital infrastructure, making AI technology accessible to local farmers and regional governments, thereby narrowing the digital divide and supporting economic growth.
